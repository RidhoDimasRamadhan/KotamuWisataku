Etiquette and Ground Rules at Tourist Sites

Indonesia holds an enormously varied set of destinations, and nearly all of them stand on land with its own owners, customs, and rules. Behaving appropriately is not merely good manners — it decides whether a place stays open to the visitors who come after you. Here are the basics that apply almost everywhere.

Dress appropriately at places of worship

Temples, mosques, and shrines are active places of worship, not photo backdrops. Cover your shoulders and knees, remove your footwear when asked, and stay out of areas reserved for people who are praying. Many temples in Bali provide a sarong and sash at the entrance; use them, even for a short visit.

Ask before photographing people

Local people's faces, traditional ceremonies, and the inside of family homes are not public property. A brief question before you raise the camera is enough. At some ceremonies photography is forbidden outright — respect that without negotiating.

Take your rubbish with you

Many natural sites in Indonesia have no adequate waste collection. The safest principle: whatever you carry in, carry back out. Do not take anything from nature either — coral, sand, edelweiss, or fragments of temple stone. Beyond the damage, some of it is against the law.

Rules for flying a drone

Drones cannot be flown just anywhere. Airport zones, vital installations, and some heritage sites prohibit them entirely, while national parks usually require written permission. Ask the site operator before you fly, not after you are told off.

Keep your distance from wildlife

Komodo dragons, the monkeys around temple grounds, and nesting turtles are not pets. Do not feed them, do not touch them, and keep to the safe distance your guide sets. Feeding wild animals changes their behaviour and often ends in conflict with people.

Support the local economy

Staying in locally owned guesthouses, eating at nearby warungs, and hiring local guides means the benefits of tourism actually reach the community that looks after the place. Bargaining is fine, but remember that a small difference to you can be a large one to them.
