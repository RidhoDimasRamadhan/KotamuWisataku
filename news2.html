Travelling Healthy and Safe

A holiday that ends at a clinic is not a successful holiday. The good news is that most health and safety problems on the road can be prevented by simple habits that cost neither much time nor much money. Here is what deserves to become routine every time you travel.

Before you leave

Make sure you are genuinely well. Postponing departure by a day is far cheaper than falling ill in an unfamiliar city. Bring personal medication in its original packaging along with a copy of the prescription if needed, and keep emergency contacts somewhere easy to reach — including on your lock screen. For long trips or higher-risk activities such as hiking and diving, travel insurance is worth considering.

While you are in transit

Sitting for hours on a plane, train, or bus slows your circulation. Stand up and walk briefly every one to two hours, and drink water more often than you would at home. Be careful with food whose preparation you cannot see, especially on long overland journeys; choose busy places where the food is still served hot.

In the outdoors

Weather in the mountains and along the coast can change within minutes. Check the forecast on the morning of departure, not a week beforehand. Wear footwear with a coarse tread for rocky or muddy trails, carry a light rain jacket, and never leave the marked path. If a site requires a guide, follow that rule — guides are not a formality, they know where the danger points are.

In the water

Wear a life jacket on a boat, even if you can swim and the crossing is short. Ask local people about currents before entering the sea, and avoid swimming alone or outside marked areas. On beaches with heavy surf, the rip current pulling back out to sea is the hazard most often underestimated.

Protecting your belongings and data

Split cash and cards between two places so that a single loss does not derail the whole trip. Avoid banking over open public Wi-Fi. Turn on device tracking, and keep photos of important documents in cloud storage as a backup.
